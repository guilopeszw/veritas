## Important distinctions

- AI: Lula’s program includes synthetic-content tracing and algorithm transparency, but this is general policy, not evidence about his campaign’s actual AI use. There is no documented campaign-specific Flávio statement in this audit. The proposed gap explicitly avoids claiming silence or absence of policy. Do not substitute a general AI policy for evidence of election advertising conduct. This candidate comparison remains incomplete.
- Bets: distinguish Lula’s 25 September government MP from Flávio’s narrower program restriction on social-benefit money and financial education. The source for Flávio is physical page 33, not the campaign page alone.
- Bolsa Família: distinguish the 17 September presidential decree from a 19 October statement by campaign coordinator Daniella Marques. Do not attribute her words directly to Flávio or say the proposal terminates all benefits after two years. Current protection rules vary by entry cohort; avoid claiming an unqualified one-year current rule.
- Security: use the official program’s five maximum-security prisons, not the separately reported 500,000 debate vacancies, whose audiovisual review remains pending. Include the CRS legal qualification if mentioning designation and military intervention.
- Economic models: the Milei reference belongs to Daniella Marques, reported by Exame; Lula’s diversification is an attributed program summary. Neither constitutes a complete economic model.
- Inflation: minimum-wage proposals are concrete connections to purchasing power, not candidate quotations about inflation falling or evidence of lower prices.
- Fiscal policy: both program examples concern spending/debt; neither establishes presidential control over monetary-policy decisions.
- Aggregate numbers: the candidate connections are explicitly proposed evaluation criteria, not invented candidate statements or observed outcomes.
