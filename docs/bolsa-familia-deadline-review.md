## Complete discrepancy ledger for this check

| Issue | Resolution and edit |
|---|---|
| 12 versus 18 months | Twelve is the historical general rule introduced by Ordinance 1,084/2025. The newer consolidated article 20 specifies 18. Both candidate cards now distinguish the two changes. |
| 18 versus 24 months | Article 54-A preserves up to 24 months for families already in protection through June 2025. It is not the general rule for everyone. |
| Two-month exception omitted by a universal deadline | Article 20 retains two months for death pensions, retirement, public-sector social-security benefits and BPC for older people. Disability BPC is not listed in that exception. |
| R$706 versus R$759 | Different cohorts: general rule versus legacy cohort. These are per-person income ceilings, not benefit amounts or the ordinary admission threshold. |
| Full benefit versus transition payment | Articles 21 and 54-A specify half the eligible benefits, rounded upward to an integer. The headline program floor does not guarantee that amount during protection. |
| 2025 versus 2026 chronology | The 18-month amendment is dated June 2026. Do not backdate it to the July 2025 reform. This edition does not infer individual retroactive entitlement or administrative reinstatement. |
| Lula only shortened the rule | Incomplete account: the MDS shortened the general rule in 2025 and lengthened it again in 2026. These are ministry acts under his government, not personal presidential signatures. |
| Flávio promises two years versus current rule | The reported coordinator proposal exceeds 18 months by six, conditional on comparable eligibility and benefit parameters. Legacy families already have up to 24. This does not establish a universal gain, full benefit, or termination for everyone after two years. |
| Current-rule uncertainty in our previous text | Removed from both cards and the shared explainer. Added the updated official PDF and reader-visible correction. Previous correction events remain historical records. |
